INDIA: AI & TECH

1. Dimension NXG launches OJAS Forward Deployed Engineering for hospital AI
Mumbai-based Dimension NXG announced OJAS Forward Deployed Engineering on 7 November, a service that embeds engineers with hospitals, health systems and government health programmes to move clinical AI from pilots into day-to-day workflows. The company says its deployments run on hospital-controlled infrastructure, behind the institution's firewall, with logged actions and clinician approval before anything is added to a patient record. The model addresses hospitals' concerns about sensitive patient data and integration complexity ahead of the DPDP Rules' compliance deadline in May 2027, although clinical performance and adoption will need to be assessed through real deployments.

2. ETHGlobal Mumbai concludes its 36-hour builder sprint with finalist judging
ETHGlobal Mumbai ran from 5–7 November, with the final day on 7 November devoted to project demonstrations and partner judging. ETHGlobal's event updates reported 1,402 hackers and 622 submitted projects, with eight finalist teams selected; the event's prize page lists tracks spanning infrastructure, payments, identity and agent-related applications. GLOBAL AI & TECHNOLOGY

3. World Media Summit adopts Beijing Consensus and launches an AI standards initiative
The seventh World Media Summit opened in Beijing on 7 November under the theme “Media in the Intelligent Age: From Consensus to Action,” bringing together nearly 500 representatives from 112 countries and regions, according to Xinhua. Delegates adopted the Beijing Consensus and launched a Global Media Initiative for the Joint Development of AI Application Standards, intended to develop practical standards for media uses of AI, protect information authenticity and data security, and uphold media ethics. The initiative is planned as a two-year collaboration; its impact will depend on participation, implementation and whether the resulting standards are independently useful across different media systems.

4. Reuters finds environmental organisations adopting AI while questioning its infrastructure costs
A Reuters report published on 7 November, based on interviews with 15 environmental, conservation and development organisations, found that all used AI in some form while also expressing concern about the electricity, water and emissions associated with data centres. Examples included satellite-based wildfire alerts, wildlife population analysis and digitisation of historic field notes; some organisations are experimenting with edge processing and limits on resource-intensive tasks. The report captures a growing tension between AI's value for climate and biodiversity work and the environmental costs of the infrastructure supporting it.
Source:
https://www.reuters.com/sustainability/cop/environmental-groups-embrace-ai-even-they-warn-data-center-impacts-2026-11-07/

5. Nova Scotia residents protest a proposed AI data centre over power and environmental concerns
Nearly 1,000 people gathered in Halifax on 7 November to oppose a proposed AI data centre in Point Tupper, Nova Scotia, while organisers said at least seven demonstrations were planned across the province, The Canadian Press reported. The project is intended to support Anthropic's Claude services and is expected by the developer to draw substantial electricity and water as it expands; provincial officials said it would need to meet environmental, water and power requirements. The protests show that AI infrastructure decisions are becoming local political issues, with energy costs, water use and community consent increasingly part of data-centre planning.

6. TCL CSOT schedules its Display Tech-Ecosystem Conference around AI-era display applications
TCL CSOT announced on 7 November that its Global Display Tech-Ecosystem Conference 2026 will take place in Wuhan on 13 November under the theme “True Vision, AI in Motion.” The company said the programme will cover inkjet-printed OLED, its APEX display platform and AI applications in content creation and human-machine interaction. The announcement is a preview of a planned industry event rather than a product launch; the significance will depend on the technical details and demonstrations disclosed at the conference.

7. PaXini demonstrates tactile sensing and humanoid manipulation at RobotWorld 2026
PaXini AI used the final day of South Korea's RobotWorld 2026, held from 4–7 November, to demonstrate tactile sensing and robotic manipulation, according to a company update distributed on 7 November. Its TORA-series humanoids performed tasks including assembling a toy rocking horse and making ice cream, while dexterous hands demonstrated tactile feedback and force sensing. These are exhibition demonstrations and company-described capabilities, not proof of reliable deployment at industrial scale, but they illustrate the role of touch and force feedback in moving embodied AI beyond vision-only interaction.
