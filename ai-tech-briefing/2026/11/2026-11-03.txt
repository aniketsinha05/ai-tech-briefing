INDIA: AI & TECH

1. IndiaAI Mission releases EKA-1 and Dhīr foundation models

The Ministry of Electronics and IT's IndiaAI Mission released EKA-1, developed by Soket Labs, and Dhīr 2B and Dhīr 5B, developed by ZenteiQ, under the Innovation Centre's foundation-model pillar. PIB says EKA-1 has 26.3 billion parameters and was trained on approximately 7.5 trillion tokens across all 22 scheduled Indian languages and English, while the open-source Dhīr models support English and 10 Indian languages with on-premise and offline deployment options. The release is a visible milestone for India's publicly supported foundation-model effort, although independent benchmark comparisons and evidence from production deployments will be important next tests.

Source: https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2332315&lang=1&reg=3

2. Route Mobile subsidiary launches AI voice-fraud platform 365callguard

365squared, a Route Mobile company within Proximus Global, launched 365callguard, an AI-powered voice-fraud protection platform for mobile and fixed-line operators and voice carriers. The company says it combines dedicated AI models with rule-based detection across seven categories, including vishing, caller-ID spoofing, Wangiri, international revenue-share fraud, SIM-box fraud, flash calls and robocalls. It can trigger call blocking, caller-ID changes or subscriber alerts depending on operator policy and network capability; the company did not publish independent detection-accuracy figures.

Source: https://routemobile.com/press-release/365squared-launches-ai-powered-voice-fraud-protection-solution-365callguard/

3. STTGDC inaugurates 22 MW AI-ready data centre in Noida

STTGDC India inaugurated NOI2, a 22 MW critical-IT-load data centre at its Noida campus, as part of approximately 200 MW of planned capacity across Delhi NCR. The company says the new facility is designed for cloud, hyperscale and AI workloads and has been selected to host the AI-compute expansion of Indian AI cloud provider Neysa. The inauguration adds physical capacity to India's data-centre market, while actual utilisation and the pace of Neysa's planned deployment will determine how quickly that capacity translates into available compute.

Source: https://www.sttgdc.com/in-en/newsroom/sttgdc-india-inaugurates-22-mw-data-centre-noida-part-approximately-200-mw-planned-across

4. Cognizant models potential AI productivity gains for India's economy

Cognizant published research estimating that AI-enabled productivity could add approximately $28 billion to $84 billion in annual nominal GDP under its realistic near-term adoption scenarios. Its survey of 700 enterprise employees and 75 senior executives found that 97% of surveyed employees reported some productivity improvement, averaging a self-reported 15%, while leaders said only 36% of employees had access to AI tools. Cognizant also calculated a theoretical upper bound of $830 billion, but explicitly says that figure is not a forecast and that all GDP estimates are directional, modelled from survey responses.

Source: https://news.cognizant.com/2026-110-03-Cognizant-study-says-productivity-gains-from-AI-could-expand-Indias-GDP

5. Apple reports record September-quarter revenue in India

Apple's fiscal 2026 fourth-quarter results, posted on its India newsroom on 3 November, showed global revenue of $114.5 billion, up 12% year over year, and record Services revenue of $31.6 billion. On the earnings call, CEO John Ternus said India also delivered an all-time September-quarter revenue record and a record for iPhone upgraders, although Apple did not disclose an India revenue total. Apple also warned that unprecedented memory-price increases would weigh on gross margins in the December quarter, linking AI-driven memory demand to costs facing consumer-device makers.

6. OpenAI says India is now ChatGPT's largest market by weekly active users

OpenAI India managing director Prabhjeet Singh told The Economic Times that India had become ChatGPT's largest country market by weekly active users and that its user base had doubled since January 2026. The interview coverage did not provide a new absolute user count, so the ranking and growth rate remain company-reported rather than independently audited figures. Singh pointed to deeper everyday use and a stronger focus on voice, Hindi and other Indian-language experiences, making localisation and accessible product design important to the next phase of adoption.

7. TRAI mandates IP-based interconnection for new telecom links

The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India published the Telecommunication Interconnection (Third Amendment) Regulations, 2026, which take effect on 1 January 2027. New interconnections established after that date must be IP-based, while existing E1/TDM-based points and traffic at older Long Distance Charging Area and Short Distance Charging Area levels must migrate in phases to IP-based and Licensed Service Area-level arrangements within three years. The changes are intended to simplify routing, improve bandwidth efficiency and strengthen call quality and network reliability.

8. NITI Aayog launches India S&T Scoreboard

NITI Aayog launched the India S&T Scoreboard, an interactive platform intended to give policymakers a consolidated view of science and technology capacity across Indian States and Union Territories. The scoreboard brings together inputs such as higher-education institutions, research laboratories, deep-tech startups and incubators with outputs including publications and patents, and adds indicators covering research quality, impact, collaboration and open access. Its value will depend on consistent data definitions, coverage and update frequency, but the platform could support more evidence-based comparison and research-policy decisions.

9. Aditya-L1 observations reveal asymmetric expansion of a solar eruption

The Indian Institute of Astrophysics used the VELC instrument aboard Aditya-L1, together with NASA's Solar Dynamics Observatory data and three-dimensional modelling, to analyse a coronal mass ejection that occurred on 5 August 2024. The study found that the eruption expanded asymmetrically rather than as a simple uniform bubble, with VELC's 530.3-nanometre spectroscopy adding line-of-sight velocity information to the imaging observations. Better estimates of a coronal mass ejection's shape, speed and arrival time can improve space-weather forecasting relevant to satellites and communications; this is a research result about an earlier event, not a solar eruption on 3 November.

Sources:
https://www.pib.gov.in/PressReleseDetailm.aspx?PRID=2331935&lang=1&reg=3

GLOBAL AI & TECHNOLOGY

10. Baidu introduces Apollo World simulation system for robotaxis

Baidu launched Apollo World, an AI simulation system designed to recreate road environments and help train autonomous vehicles. Baidu says it can generate up to 100,000 kilometres of simulated driving per day, along with camera and lidar data, and is already being used to train its Apollo Go robotaxi fleet. The claimed simulation scale could reduce the cost of testing rare or complex driving scenarios, but it does not by itself establish real-world safety or commercial readiness.

Source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/chinas-baidu-launches-ai-model-simulating-road-environment-robotaxis-2026-11-03/

11. Bull and Schwarz Digits sign European sovereign AI infrastructure MoU

French high-performance-computing company Bull and Schwarz Digits, the IT arm of Schwarz Group, signed a memorandum of understanding to develop a European sovereign AI and cloud infrastructure stack. The plan combines Bull's server and HPC expertise with Schwarz Digits' STACKIT cloud, including rack-scale AI systems, direct-liquid-cooling capabilities and the planned availability of BullSequana AI software through the STACKIT marketplace. This is a preliminary partnership rather than a delivered service, but it reflects Europe's effort to retain greater control over compute, data and infrastructure used by regulated sectors.

Source: https://www.bull.com/en/press-releases/pr-bull-and-schwarz-digits-a-franco-german-alliance-for-europes-digital-sovereign?hs_amp=true

12. CrowdStrike expands security controls for enterprise AI agents

At Fal.Con Europe, CrowdStrike introduced an AI Kill Switch in Falcon Guardian that lets authorised defenders terminate unauthorised agents at the Gateway today, block them from relaunching, and add endpoint-level termination in a later update. It also signed definitive agreements to acquire agent-observability firm Fiddler and AI security-testing company Dreadnode, and announced a native Falcon Guardian integration for Anthropic's Claude Cowork. The acquisitions remain subject to closing conditions, but the package aims to give enterprises pre-deployment testing, runtime control and auditability for agents.

13. Google adds Singapore research hub and more than $6 million in university grants

Google said it has established a research hub in Singapore and is providing more than $6 million in grants and capacity-building support involving the National University of Singapore and Nanyang Technological University. The work spans AI for science, Alzheimer's research, computing efficiency, regional-language processing, AI safety and trustworthy multi-agent systems; Google also announced separate funding for INSEAD's Thrive Lab. The commitments are ecosystem investments rather than a new consumer product, with potential benefits for regional research capacity and safer, more efficient AI systems.

Source: https://blog.google/innovation-and-ai/technology/research/partnering-singapore-advance-scientific-discovery/

14. Google changes AI controls for schools and brings Guided Learning to Search and Classroom

Google announced that Guided Learning is globally available in Google Classroom and will be piloted in Search at selected US schools later in November. It also said Gemini, Gemini Notebook, Gemini in Classroom and Google Search will be off for K-12 student accounts until school administrators explicitly enable them. The Verge reported concern that administrators may not be able to retain Search while turning off its Gemini-powered experience, while Google said school administrators can control services individually; the change puts school-level AI governance and product access in sharper focus.

15. UK NCSC warns AI will accelerate cyber operations and ransomware capability

The UK's National Cyber Security Centre published its Annual Review 2026, assessing that AI is highly likely to increase the speed, scale and efficiency of hostile cyber operations, including reconnaissance, social engineering, vulnerability discovery and exploit development. The agency says AI is more likely to accelerate and scale existing tactics than generate new attack vectors, and assesses that AI malware is highly likely to be used in a ransomware attack against UK critical national infrastructure. These are the NCSC's threat judgments and forecasts, not a report that such an attack has already occurred.

Sources:

16. Binance details how fake recruitment and an internal AI tool were used in a $6.3 billion theft

Binance's initial detailed account of its 29 October security breach, published on 3 November, said attackers used malware delivered through a tampered developer tool and a fake job interview to compromise engineers. The exchange said attackers later used an internal AI tool on 20 October to access wallet developers' systems before stolen credentials were used in the 29 October theft; Binance said approximately $2.85 billion in assets had been frozen or intercepted and that affected customers would be reimbursed. The breach itself predates this coverage date, so the 3 November development is the forensic disclosure; attribution to a North Korean group remains disputed.

17. Xiaomi tests its embodied AI model in a real apartment setting

Xiaomi said its Xiaomi-Robotics-1 embodied model has entered trial operation at the company's Youth Apartments, where an agent system completed more than 30 minutes of work across three rooms and more than 50 household tasks. The reported tasks included desk tidying, rubbish collection, sorting and storing objects, room inspections and a final home check; IT Home cited a video posted by Xiaomi Technology. The trial is limited to Xiaomi's own apartment environment, and the company has not published independent success-rate or unattended-operation data, so it is a useful deployment test rather than proof of general-purpose home-robot readiness.

Source: https://www.ithome.com/1/021/602.htm

18. Trajectory raises $45 million to automate AI model post-training

AI startup Trajectory announced a $45 million Series A led by Sequoia at a $300 million valuation, with Nvidia, Bessemer Venture Partners and Bain Capital Ventures participating, and opened its post-training platform. The product connects feedback from business applications to continual fine-tuning of open models for specialised tasks, with the company naming Harvey, Rogo, Decagon, Mercor and Nvidia among its customers. The funding, valuation and customer list are company-reported, and independent performance data were not provided; the announcement's 2 November afternoon timestamp in Pacific Time falls on 3 November in India.
