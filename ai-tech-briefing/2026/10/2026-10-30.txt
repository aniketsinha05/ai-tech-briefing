INDIA: AI & TECH

1. TRAI proposes sector-specific responsible-AI guidelines for telecom

The Telecom Regulatory Authority of India released a draft consultation paper on responsible AI use in telecom networks and services on 30 October. It proposes board-approved internal AI policies, risk classification of systems, human oversight, AI incident recording and reporting, supplier due diligence, and validation before deployment; stakeholders have until 27 November to comment. The document is a draft for consultation, not a final binding framework, but it could become an important sector-specific layer over India's broader AI governance approach.

2. IIT Guwahati and Tata Electronics establish semiconductor-packaging education partnership

IIT Guwahati said on 30 October that it had signed an MoU with Tata Electronics to establish a Centre of Excellence focused on semiconductor Assembly, Testing, Marking and Packaging (ATMP). The partnership is intended to develop diploma- and B.Tech.-level training, hands-on learning, faculty development and joint research, including opportunities for students from colleges and polytechnics across Assam and the Northeast. It addresses a critical part of India's semiconductor ambitions: building technicians and engineers who can support packaging and manufacturing operations, not only chip design.

3. Digital Bharat Nidhi signs Tamil Nadu BharatNet agreement for rural fibre

The Department of Telecommunications' Digital Bharat Nidhi, the Tamil Nadu government and TANFINET signed an agreement on 30 October to support use of the BharatNet network under the Amended BharatNet Programme. The government says the arrangement is expected to support more than five lakh rural home-fibre connections and provide on-demand connectivity to 4,767 villages. If implementation keeps pace, the additional last-mile capacity could widen access to e-governance, online education, telemedicine and digital payments in rural areas.

4. Jio Platforms reports 13.7% profit growth in first results after listing

Jio Platforms reported a 13.7% year-on-year rise in quarterly net profit to ₹8,389 crore for the quarter ended September, while revenue from operations increased 11.4% to ₹40,475 crore. The company added 9.1 million net subscribers during the quarter, and managing director Akash Ambani said Jio would continue investing in satellite communications, 6G intellectual property and AI integrated into voice and data services. The results provide an early public-market view of the scale and investment priorities of India's largest telecom operator.

5. Netpoleon India and QNu Labs partner on quantum-safe enterprise security

Netpoleon India announced a collaboration with Indian deep-tech company QNu Labs to expand enterprise access to quantum-safe cybersecurity and post-quantum cryptography readiness. The partnership combines QNu Labs' QShield 2.0 platform—covering cryptographic risk profiling, operations and governance—with Netpoleon's channel-partner and systems-integrator network. The companies are targeting sectors such as banking, government, defence and telecom; the announcement describes a route to market, not evidence that large-scale deployments have already taken place.

6. CloudSEK launches cybersecurity incubator and KeyVigil AI gateway

CloudSEK's 30 October announcement introduced Launchpad, a 12-week incubator that plans to select 10 early-stage cybersecurity startups, each eligible for up to US$5,000 in AI-model credits, mentorship, threat-intelligence resources and enterprise introductions. Alongside it, the company launched KeyVigil, a gateway for applications and AI agents to access multiple frontier models through one endpoint, with centralised API-key management, sensitive-data masking, prompt-injection and jailbreak detection, interaction logs and spending limits. The combination targets two gaps in the AI-security market: helping security startups reach customers and giving enterprises more control over how their own applications and agents use models.

7. GitHub points to public-sector demand in India's expanding AI market

In an interview published on 30 October, GitHub's Asia-Pacific, India and Japan vice-president Sharryn Napier said government agencies and public-sector organisations are becoming important sources of AI demand in India. She said the Indian government is already a GitHub customer and cited roughly 32 million developers on the platform in India, up 30% year on year; potential use cases include citizen-service chatbots and grievance-redressal systems. The opportunity is substantial, but the report also highlights the prerequisites for deployment at scale: reliable data, compute, cybersecurity, skilled developers and clear rules for data storage and ownership.

GLOBAL AI & TECHNOLOGY

8. OpenAI announces new community-safety reporting and stronger protections for teens

OpenAI published a Community Safety Report commitment and announced tighter limits on weapons-related information available to minors on 30 October. It also said ChatGPT would provide less detailed reconstructions of past mass attacks for all users, while building on systems that use contextual warning signs and age prediction to respond more conservatively when violence risk is suspected. The company says the new safeguards will begin rolling out over the next few weeks; the first public report is planned for early 2027 and will cover the second half of 2026.

Source:
https://openai.com/index/community-safety-report-and-new-protections-for-teens/

9. Claude Code 2.1.314 adds a hard boundary against out-of-project writes

Anthropic's Claude Code 2.1.314 release, published on 30 October, adds a setting that makes the Write, Edit and NotebookEdit tools refuse paths outside the configured working directories in every permission mode. The release also extends idle conversation compaction to VS Code and the Code tab in Claude Desktop, adds identity-provider group routing for Claude apps gateway upstreams, and improves Bedrock guardrail tracing. The write restriction is particularly relevant to teams running coding agents with broad or reduced-confirmation permissions because it provides an explicit filesystem boundary for the file-editing tools.

https://github.com/anthropics/claude-code/commit/b3f291d

10. Google Cloud's October roundup highlights infrastructure built for AI agents

Google Cloud published its October AI-infrastructure roundup on 30 October, bringing together a month of launches and updates for agentic workloads. Highlights include Agent Compute Engine with isolated microVM sandboxes and governance controls, the open-source llm-d 1.0 serving stack, TorchTPU for native PyTorch work on Google TPUs, and ML Drift for on-device GPU inference; the roundup also describes air-gapped Gemini search and support for NVIDIA HGX B300 systems. The post is a monthly recap, so these components were introduced at different points during October rather than all launching on 30 October.

12. Binance details a $6.3 billion security incident and reserves funds to cover losses

Binance said on 30 October that unauthorised withdrawals from its hot wallets had affected approximately US$6.3 billion in token assets. The exchange said about US$3 billion had been frozen with industry partners and that it had set aside more than US$6.34 billion in company funds to cover the impact; its preliminary investigation pointed to a potentially compromised DevOps engineer's laptop, while it said it had found no evidence of leaked wallet private keys. The incident's reported start time was 29 October at 20:05 UTC, which was 30 October in India, and the full attack path remains subject to investigation.

13. Investors question the returns on Big Tech's accelerating AI spending

Reuters reported on 30 October that the Nasdaq 100 was on course to lose more than US$2 trillion in market value during October, with the index down over 5.8% for the month as investors reassessed the returns from hyperscalers' AI infrastructure spending. Higher capital-expenditure forecasts, rising borrowing costs and growing use of debt to finance infrastructure have sharpened concerns about the time required to monetise AI investments. The picture is not uniformly negative: Reuters also noted continued revenue strength in cloud and other major businesses, leaving the market focused on the gap between rising demand and the pace of financial returns.

14. Tokyo Electron forecasts 87% operating-profit growth on chip-equipment demand

Tokyo Electron forecast an 87% increase in operating profit to 1.17 trillion yen, a stronger outlook than analysts had expected, citing robust demand for semiconductor manufacturing equipment. The company also said it would spend up to 150 billion yen on a share buyback, while other Asian chip suppliers continued to report strong AI-related demand despite market volatility. The update offers a useful counterpoint to the selloff in AI-linked equities: investors are questioning returns on the overall build-out, but suppliers are still seeing demand for the equipment needed to manufacture chips.

15. Vatican meeting calls for international institutions to oversee AI

Pope Leo XIV told an AI gathering at the Vatican on 30 October that the urgent task is to create institutions capable of putting ethical principles into practice. The discussion, organised by the Pontifical Academy for Life and the Future of Life Institute, called for shared standards, independent oversight and clear accountability, while stressing that decisions affecting human life, freedom and the use of force must remain under effective human supervision. The meeting adds another voice to the growing debate over how broad AI principles can be translated into enforceable governance.

16. Nature paper proposes a framework for learning effectively with AI

A perspective published in Communications Psychology on 30 October proposes the SYNC model for understanding how people learn and perform when working with generative AI. The authors argue that AI can improve immediate task performance while weakening durable learning if users hand over planning, monitoring and evaluation; they distinguish this “metacognitive offloading” from deliberate practices that keep learners actively checking and directing their work. The paper is a conceptual framework rather than a new controlled experiment, but it offers a useful lens for schools and workplaces deciding how to use AI without confusing polished outputs with lasting competence.

Source:
https://www.nature.com/articles/s44271-026-00548-y
