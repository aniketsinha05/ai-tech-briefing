INDIA: AI & TECH

1. Jio Platforms makes a record-breaking market debut as it highlights its technology ambitions
Reliance Jio Platforms rose sharply on its first trading day after a roughly $3.2 billion IPO, reaching a reported market value of about $137 billion and becoming India's second-most valuable listed company. At the listing, managing director Akash Ambani described ambitions spanning AI, cloud computing, connected devices, space-based connectivity and digital services. The debut gives public-market investors a clearer way to value Reliance's telecom and digital business, while the next test is how those broader technology ambitions translate into products and revenue.

2. Google Cloud showcases production-oriented AI for Indian small and mid-sized businesses
Google Cloud's Cloud Day India 2026 in New Delhi brought together more than 600 founders, business leaders and technology executives to demonstrate AI tools for productivity, analytics and workflow automation. The event focused on practical adoption in sectors including manufacturing, retail, logistics, media and financial services, where smaller firms often lack large in-house AI teams. Demonstrations and vendor claims are useful signals, but adoption will ultimately depend on cost, reliability, data governance and measurable business outcomes.
Source: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/technology/tech-news/cloud-day-india-2026-google-cloud-brings-production-ready-ai-to-indias-small-and-mid-sized-businesses/articleshow/135267829.cms

3. HCLTech and AWS introduce a smaller AI-focused Global Capability Centre model
HCLTech announced an AI-powered “nano GCC” offering built on Amazon Web Services to help enterprises move from AI pilots to production workflows. The model combines HCLTech's advisory and AI Force platforms with AWS infrastructure and services including Amazon Bedrock, Kiro and AWS Transform, using compact, product-focused teams. The initial rollout is planned for Asia Pacific; the model's value will depend on whether customers can demonstrate repeatable outcomes without taking on the cost and complexity of a traditional large capability centre.
Source: https://www.hcltech.com/en-us/press-releases/hcltech-launches-ai-powered-nano-global-capability-centers-aws-help-enterprises

4. LTM launches GCC Origin for AI-native capability centres in India
LTM announced GCC Origin, a dedicated business unit designed to help enterprises establish, operate, scale and transform Global Capability Centres in India. It brings together strategy, AI, technology, talent, infrastructure and operations, and uses the company's BlueVerse ecosystem of AI agents as part of its service offering. The launch reflects a shift in the GCC market from labour-cost savings toward building business capabilities and embedding AI into operating models from the outset; the company's agent and productivity claims remain vendor-reported.
Source: https://www.ltm.com/news-events/press-releases/2026/ltm-launches-gcc-for-next-generation-capability-centers

5. Bengaluru enterprise-AI startup QwikBuild raises $2.3 million
QwikBuild raised a $2.3 million pre-seed round led by Japan-listed Globalway, with Blume Ventures, DeVC, Marsshot and angel investors also reported among the participants. The startup develops enterprise software and AI-agent workflows intended to help businesses build applications or add custom layers around existing systems. The funding is primarily intended to support go-to-market expansion, while a planned joint venture with Globalway offers a route into Japan's enterprise software market; customer and performance figures are company-reported.
Source: https://www.startupresearcher.com/news/qwikbuild-raises-us-2-3-million-pre-seed

GLOBAL AI & TECHNOLOGY

6. Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon with a cybersecurity focus
Google introduced Gemini 4 Carbon, a new frontier model positioned for advanced coding and cybersecurity work, following Gemini 4 Argon earlier in October. Reuters reported the launch but noted that detailed capability and availability information was not yet provided. Independent evaluations, technical documentation and evidence of how access is controlled will be important for assessing the model's real-world impact, particularly in dual-use security tasks.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/google-launches-gemini-4-carbon-ai-model-with-cybersecurity-focus-2026-10-28/

7. New York signs restrictions on AI companion chatbots interacting with minors
New York Governor Kathy Hochul signed a law restricting certain chatbot behaviours when AI companions interact with people under 18, including presenting themselves as real people with emotions or relationships and encouraging secrecy or unhealthy behaviour. The law is scheduled to take effect on 1 January and gives the state attorney general enforcement options. Its implementation will matter beyond New York because developers may need to adapt product design, age assurance and safety controls across jurisdictions; some provisions have been criticised as broad or difficult to interpret.
Source: https://apnews.com/article/03a4e090a7df1532838a5cb6f93a23ba

8. SAP is shifting suitable workloads to cheaper AI models to manage costs
SAP CFO Dominik Asam told Reuters that the company is replacing some expensive AI models with lower-cost alternatives when they deliver adequate performance. SAP uses more than 70 models from providers including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, and says its broader AI-driven efficiency programme could generate more than €2 billion in gains. The strategy highlights a growing enterprise priority: selecting models by task-level quality and cost rather than defaulting to the most powerful model, while overall AI spending may still rise as usage expands.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/china/sap-swaps-costly-ai-models-cheaper-alternatives-targets-over-2-bln-gains-2026-10-28/

9. Microsoft reports Azure revenue of $33.2 billion as cloud demand remains strong
Microsoft reported that Azure generated about $33.2 billion in revenue for the July–September quarter, up 48% year over year, according to TechCrunch's analysis of the company's results. Microsoft itself confirmed 48% Azure growth and reported $63.9 billion in total Microsoft Cloud revenue, up 30%. Azure's disclosure makes comparison with other cloud providers easier, but the figures cover a broad cloud business and should not be read as AI revenue alone.

10. Microsoft discloses $10 billion in quarterly revenue tied to OpenAI
Microsoft reported $10 billion in revenue from commercial arrangements with OpenAI for the quarter ended 30 September, including revenue-sharing payments, according to a regulatory filing reported by TechCrunch. That was nearly 11% of Microsoft's $92.3 billion quarterly revenue, underscoring the scale of the companies' financial ties even as they increasingly compete for customers and OpenAI works with additional cloud providers. The filing did not break out how much came from Azure services versus revenue sharing, so the components should not be inferred from the headline figure.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/microsoft-recorded-10-billion-in-revenue-from-openai/

11. Anthropic adds enterprise tools to show how teams use and spend on AI
Anthropic introduced reporting tools for Claude Enterprise that let managers review team-level AI usage, work produced, costs and points where workflows break down. The company says the reports use internal analytics and employee surveys and that this data will not be used for model training by default; pooled budgets are also intended to help teams manage token spending. The launch responds to growing pressure on enterprises to demonstrate return on investment from AI tools, though usage and cost reports alone do not prove productivity gains.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/anthropic-just-launched-tools-to-show-companies-what-their-ai-spending-is-buying/

12. Anthropic expands its AI-for-science programme to 20 research organisations
Anthropic highlighted 20 scientific foundations, institutions and research groups joining an expansion of its AI for Science programme, which provides Claude credits to researchers working on high-impact projects. The company says researchers are using AI to develop hypotheses, design experiments, analyse complex data and communicate results, and that some collaborations aim to produce shared datasets, open-source software and standards. These are research-enablement efforts rather than proof that AI has already delivered specific scientific breakthroughs; outcomes will need to be judged by the quality and reproducibility of the resulting work.
Source: https://www.anthropic.com/news/ai-for-science-expands-foundations-research-institutions

13. Google expands advertising inside AI-powered Search experiences internationally
Google said it is expanding ads in AI Mode into international markets and adding multi-turn ad experiences in the United States. The announcement came with Alphabet's quarterly results, which showed $127.6 billion in revenue while Search advertising growth slowed to 12% year over year; Google said early sponsored-product click-through rates in AI Mode were comparable to organic listings. The rollout is an important test of how conversational search can be monetised, and whether sponsored recommendations remain clearly distinguishable from AI-generated answers.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/google-expands-ai-search-ads-globally-as-core-ad-growth-slows/

14. US Justice Department asks questions about Nvidia's proposed Hugging Face acquisition
Reuters reported that the US Department of Justice has made inquiries about how Nvidia's proposed $12.93 billion acquisition of Hugging Face could affect competition in AI, including whether Nvidia could gain control over a major model and developer distribution channel. The questions are part of broader scrutiny of Nvidia's acquisitions, investments and sales practices; an inquiry is not a finding of wrongdoing and does not mean the deal will necessarily be blocked. The outcome could matter to developers who rely on Hugging Face to share models, datasets and tools across competing hardware and cloud platforms.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/us/us-doj-is-inquiring-about-nvidias-purchase-hugging-face-sources-say-2026-10-28/

15. Cloud-security startup Upwind raises $400 million
Upwind raised a $400 million Series C round at a reported $3.8 billion valuation, led by Bessemer Venture Partners, according to TechCrunch. The company focuses on runtime cloud security, detecting and responding to threats and vulnerabilities in active services, an area gaining attention as cloud infrastructure and AI workloads grow more complex. The valuation and revenue trajectory cited in the report are not independently audited here, but the size of the round signals strong investor interest in security products built for dynamic cloud environments.
Source: https://techcrunch.com/2026/10/28/upwind-raises-400m-after-walking-away-from-datadog-acquisition-offer/

16. AI-assisted molecule screening points to a possible new route for quantum-computing chips
German startup NVision published research in Nature describing an approach that uses a thin layer of an organic molecule on a conventionally manufactured chip as a potential component for quantum computing. Reuters reported that AI helped screen hundreds of candidate molecules before researchers synthesised and tested a smaller set; NVision is working with US chipmaker HyperLight on its first chips. The result is an early research path, not a commercially ready quantum computer, but it could offer a different route to fabricating specialised quantum hardware.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/german-startup-nvision-shows-new-path-to-quantum-computing-chips-nature-paper-2026-10-28/

17. China's CXMT prepares limited HBM3E production despite low yields
Reuters reported that China's largest DRAM maker, ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), plans to begin limited production of HBM3E memory used in AI processors within about two months, despite reported yields of only 20%–30%. The company has sent samples to Chinese AI-chip designers, but testing reportedly suggests performance remains closer to an earlier generation of high-bandwidth memory than to leading HBM3E products. The effort reflects China's drive to reduce reliance on foreign AI hardware, while low yields and manufacturing constraints remain major obstacles to scaling competitive memory.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-cxmt-start-advanced-ai-memory-production-despite-poor-yields-sources-say-2026-10-28/

18. China proposes tighter export controls for advanced AI technologies
Reuters reported that China has proposed adding 23 AI-related technologies to restricted or prohibited export lists, including advanced models, autonomous agents, chip-design tools and systems used to operate large computing facilities. The draft would generally allow overseas customers to use Chinese AI models through websites or cloud platforms while requiring government approval for certain transfers of underlying technology, data or expertise; public comments are open until 15 November. The proposal could affect Chinese AI companies' international expansion and technology partnerships, but its final scope will depend on the consultation and any adopted rules.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/retail-consumer/chinas-new-export-curbs-draw-line-around-its-ai-crown-jewels-2026-10-28/
