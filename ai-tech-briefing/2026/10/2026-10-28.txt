1. Jio Platforms lists strongly and frames AI, cloud and space connectivity as its next phase
Jio Platforms made its stock-market debut at a reported 21.3% premium to its IPO price. At the listing, managing director Akash Ambani described the next phase as making AI, cloud computing, connected devices and space-based connectivity more accessible across India. The ambition is broad rather than a detailed product roadmap, so the key follow-up is whether Jio publishes concrete offerings, rollout dates and pricing.
Source: https://economictimes.indiatimes.com/markets/stocks/news/akash-ambani-announces-jios-next-big-mission-after-data-bring-ai-cloud-and-space-connectivity-to-every-indian/articleshow/135252824.cms

2. Google Cloud pitches production AI to Indian small and mid-sized businesses
At Cloud Day India 2026 in New Delhi, Google Cloud gathered more than 600 business and technology leaders and demonstrated AI tools for productivity, workflow automation and business analytics. Examples discussed included demand forecasting across more than 40,000 products at apparel company Siyaram's, as well as AI-assisted marketing content and workflows. The practical question for Indian SMBs is whether integrated tools can deliver measurable savings and reliable outcomes without requiring large in-house AI teams.
Source: https://enterpriseai.economictimes.indiatimes.com/amp/news/industry/google-cloud-showcases-ai-tools-to-help-indian-smbs-boost-productivity-automate-operations/135266942

3. HCLTech and AWS launch an AI-powered “nano GCC” model
HCLTech announced a nano Global Capability Centre offering built on Amazon Web Services, intended to help companies move AI projects beyond pilots. It combines HCLTech's GCC advisory and AI Force platform with AWS infrastructure and agentic-AI services. The model targets both established GCCs seeking an AI-led transformation and mid-market companies that want to enter a new geography without building a large operation up front; HCLTech says the initial rollout will focus on Asia Pacific.
Source: https://www.hcltech.com/en-us/press-releases/hcltech-launches-ai-powered-nano-global-capability-centers-aws-help-enterprises

4. LTM creates GCC Origin to help enterprises build AI-native capability centres in India
LTM announced GCC Origin as a standalone business unit for setting up, operating and transforming Global Capability Centres in India. It brings together site strategy, technology, AI, talent, infrastructure and operations, and uses LTM's BlueVerse agentic-AI ecosystem, which the company says includes more than 300 pre-built domain-specific agents. The announcement reflects a shift in the GCC market from labour-cost savings toward owning business capabilities and embedding AI in operating models from the start.
Source: https://www.ltm.com/news-events/press-releases/2026/ltm-launches-gcc-for-next-generation-capability-centers

5. Bengaluru enterprise-AI startup QwikBuild raises $2.3 million
QwikBuild raised a $2.3 million pre-seed round led by Japan-listed Globalway, with Blume Ventures, DeVC, Marsshot and angel investors also reported among the backers. The startup builds enterprise software and AI-agent workflows designed to help businesses create or extend applications around existing systems. The funding is intended mainly to support go-to-market expansion; reports also describe a planned Japan joint venture with Globalway, though that JV agreement had been announced earlier in October.
Source: https://cio.eletsonline.com/artificial-intelligence/qwikbuild-2-3-million-pre-seed-globalway-blume-enterprise-ai-agents-japan-jv/76485/

6. Nova Spectral AI raises ₹21.15 crore to develop an indigenous infrared sensor
Bengaluru-based Nova Spectral AI raised ₹21.15 crore in seed funding led by Shastra VC, with Equirus InnovateX Fund and angel investors participating, according to the report. It is developing a quantum-dot short-wave infrared image-sensor chip on a silicon-compatible manufacturing approach, targeting uses such as drones, surveillance and other machine-vision systems. The startup says it aims to produce a field-ready prototype within 18 months; that is a target, not a completed capability, and the technology still needs to demonstrate performance and manufacturability.
Source: https://www.peoplematters.in/news/ai-and-emerging-tech/nova-spectral-ai-raises-over-rs-21-cr-in-funding-led-by-shastra-vc-53009

7. IIT Hyderabad-incubated WiSig demonstrates indigenous 5G and pre-6G work
WiSig Networks showcased communication technologies to Prime Minister Narendra Modi at the Emerging Science, Technology and Innovation Conclave in New Delhi. Demonstrations included rural 5G connectivity, satellite-enabled services and Koala Tag, a device intended to let people in remote areas send messages, share location and seek emergency help through mobile-tower and satellite links. The company also briefed officials on OTFDM, a waveform being explored in pre-6G research; the report does not provide independent performance benchmarks or deployment commitments.
Source: https://timesofindia.indiatimes.com/city/hyderabad/iith-incubated-wisig-networks-showcases-5g-6g-innovations-to-pm-modi-at-emerging-tech-conclave/articleshow/135269138.cms

8. Google unveils Gemini 4 Carbon, a model focused on coding and cybersecurity
Google introduced Gemini 4 Carbon as a frontier model aimed at complex engineering work and cybersecurity tasks, including finding vulnerabilities and helping patch software. Reuters reports that Google claims Carbon generates secure code at more than twice the success rate of Gemini 4 Argon on internal benchmarks. Initial access is limited to selected cybersecurity defenders through Google's Fairwind Program while safeguards are tested; broad public availability was not announced.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/google-launches-gemini-4-carbon-ai-model-with-cybersecurity-focus-2026-10-28/

9. SAP is routing some AI tasks to cheaper models to improve unit economics
SAP told Reuters it is replacing some costly models in its products with cheaper alternatives where they meet the required quality bar. The company uses more than 70 models from providers including OpenAI, Anthropic and Google, and says the approach is expected to help generate more than €2 billion in AI-driven efficiency gains while overall AI spending continues to rise. The broader lesson for enterprise AI is that model choice may increasingly be made per task, balancing quality, latency, cost and customer restrictions rather than defaulting to the most expensive model.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/china/sap-swaps-costly-ai-models-cheaper-alternatives-targets-over-2-bln-gains-2026-10-28/

10. Meta pauses new commercial terms after publishers raise AI-content concerns
Meta said it would indefinitely delay rolling out proposed commercial terms after publishers and digital advertisers objected to language they believed could give Meta broad rights to use their content in AI services. The company says it will consult industry groups before changing its policies, and the existing terms remain in effect while discussions continue. The dispute highlights the unresolved legal and commercial questions around publisher content, AI training and product use, especially as regulators in Europe and the UK receive complaints.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/media-telecom/meta-delays-indefinitely-its-rollout-new-commercial-terms-after-publishers-2026-10-28/

11. China's CXMT plans limited HBM3E production despite low yields, Reuters reports
China's largest DRAM maker, ChangXin Memory Technologies (CXMT), plans to begin limited production of HBM3E memory used alongside AI processors within the next two months, according to people cited by Reuters. Reported yields are only around 20%–30%, compared with about 70% at market leader SK Hynix, and early testing reportedly puts CXMT's performance closer to the previous HBM3 generation. The move underscores China's drive to secure domestic AI-memory supply despite export controls, while also showing the technical and cost gap that remains.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/world/china/chinas-cxmt-start-advanced-ai-memory-production-despite-poor-yields-sources-say-2026-10-28/

12. AI-assisted molecule screening points to a possible new quantum-chip manufacturing route
German startup NVision published a Nature paper describing an approach that uses organic molecules deposited as a thin layer on conventionally manufactured chips. Researchers used AI to screen hundreds of candidate molecules, synthesized around 40, and identified a candidate suitable for the proposed process; NVision says it is partnering with US chipmaker HyperLight to make its first chips. This is an early research and development pathway, not a ready-to-buy quantum computer, but it could connect molecular engineering with more scalable chip fabrication.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/technology/german-startup-nvision-shows-new-path-quantum-computing-chips-nature-paper-2026-10-28/

13. Fragment launches with $56 million to automate enterprise procurement exceptions
US enterprise-AI startup Fragment launched with $56 million in reported total funding, including a Series A led by 8VC and a seed round co-led by General Catalyst and A*. Its system connects information across enterprise applications and uses company-specific policies and context to investigate and resolve purchasing and payment exceptions, rather than merely flagging them for employees. The product is aimed at large manufacturers and distributors; its promise depends on safe authorisation boundaries, accurate handling of exceptions and integration with existing systems.
Source: https://www.globenewswire.com/news-release/2026/10/28/3390260/0/en/fragment-launches-with-56-million-to-automate-the-work-enterprise-software-leaves-behind.html

14. Easol raises $30 million and expands its AI platform for experience businesses
Experience-commerce platform Easol announced a $30 million Series A extension—$20 million in equity and a $10 million debt facility—bringing its reported total funding to $60 million. Its new “Teammates” capability lets experience businesses build AI agents that use live business data for support, sales and operations across bookings, payments, marketing and customer information. The launch illustrates how agentic AI is being packaged into vertical software; the meaningful test will be whether it improves conversion, service and operational performance for customers.
Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/experience-commerce-platform-easol-raises-30-million-launches-total-ai-solution-for-experience-businesses-302921596.html

15. Ceva announces a new NPU family spanning edge AI and data-centre infrastructure
Chip-IP company Ceva unveiled a next-generation NeuPro neural-processing-unit portfolio covering always-on sensing, edge devices, robotics and AI infrastructure. Ceva claims up to twice the AI throughput per unit area and 50% better energy efficiency than its first generation; those figures are company-reported comparisons, not independent benchmark results. The portfolio's NeuPro Ultra is designed for cloud and data-centre inference as well as data-movement functions, reflecting the growing push to improve efficiency beyond simply adding more GPUs.
Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/ceva-unveils-next-generation-neupro-npu-portfolio-and-announces-expansion-into-ai-infrastructure-302921417.html

16. Akeneo adds more agentic automation to product-data management and acquires Dataggo
Akeneo announced its Autumn Release 2026, adding capabilities intended to let AI agents enrich product records, onboard supplier data and publish product information across commerce channels under enterprise access and security controls. The company also announced the acquisition of Dataggo, a specialist in AI-native product-information-management services. The development matters for retailers and manufacturers because agentic commerce depends on accurate, well-governed product data—not only on the language model or shopping interface.
Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/akeneo-puts-ai-agents-to-work-across-product-data-with-enterprise-control-built-in-302921338.html

17. Scale AI faces European privacy complaints over its data-labeling platform
Scale AI rejected complaints filed by privacy group noyb in France, Italy, the Netherlands and Austria, calling the allegations unfounded. The complaints allege that Scale's Outlier platform unlawfully monitors more than 900,000 freelancers who help produce training data; Scale says its practices are disclosed and that some project-specific screen and time recording is opt-in. The claims are allegations, not findings by regulators, but the case raises questions about worker consent, transparency and monitoring practices in the human-data supply chain behind AI systems.
Source: https://www.reuters.com/business/scale-ai-rejects-claims-it-illegally-surveils-ai-training-workers-2026-10-28/

18. Consortium forms around an open evidence protocol for AI-agent transactions
A-Comm Technologies announced a consortium to steward its A-Comm Evidence Protocol, an open standard intended to create cryptographically verifiable records across agent-led commerce—from discovery through payment authorisation and fulfilment. The founding group spans payment, compliance, dispute-resolution, identity and point-of-sale businesses. The aim is to make automated transactions more auditable when agents act across multiple services; the protocol's usefulness will depend on adoption, interoperability and how clearly it allocates responsibility when a transaction goes wrong.
Source: https://www.prnewswire.com/news-releases/a-comm-technologies-inc-forms-founding-consortium-to-establish-open-source-evidence-protocol-for-agentic-commerce-302922270.html
